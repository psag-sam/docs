**Settings → Developer & API** is where administrators let other systems use the PearlCRM API. Each system gets its own **OAuth client**: a client ID and secret, the scopes it may ask for, and the role it works under.

<Note>
  This page needs the **API Access & Integrations** add-on. Without it the tile shows as locked; ask your PearlCRM account manager to add it. You also need the **Manage integrations** permission (system administrators have it until your organisation switches on role enforcement).
</Note>

## Create a client

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Developer & API">
    Go to **Settings** and choose **Developer & API**, then **New client**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Use the name of the system that will connect, such as "Data warehouse" or "Xero sync". The name appears in the activity log and on records the integration creates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the client type">
    - **Confidential** for software that runs on a server and can keep a secret.
    - **Public** for a browser, mobile or desktop app that cannot keep a secret. It has no secret; PKCE protects its sign-in instead.

    You cannot change the type later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose how it gets tokens">
    - **Server to server (client credentials)** for a system that works on behalf of your organisation rather than a particular person. Confidential clients only.
    - **Acting for a signed-in user (authorization code + PKCE)** for an app your people connect to their own PearlCRM account. Each person approves it once and it then works as them. Enter the app's **redirect URIs**, exactly as the developer gives them.
    - **Refresh tokens** keeps an authorization-code app connected without asking the person again. The app must also be allowed the `offline_access` scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the integration user's role (server to server only)">
    A server-to-server client works as its own **integration user**, which has the role you pick here. Choose or create a role with only the access the integration needs: for a reporting export, read-only access to the objects it exports.

    The role applies to everything the integration does, even if your organisation does not yet enforce roles for its people. Administrator roles cannot be given to an integration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose scopes">
    Scopes say what the client may ask for, per object: **Read**, **Create and edit**, **Delete**. Tick only what it needs. Both the scope and the role must allow an action for it to happen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and copy the secret">
    After you choose **Create client**, PearlCRM shows the **client ID** and **client secret** once. Copy both into the other system or your secret manager. PearlCRM keeps only a fingerprint of the secret and cannot show it again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The integration user

The integration user is a PearlCRM user that exists only for this client:

- it cannot sign in to PearlCRM and has no password;
- it does not use a licence seat;
- it does not appear in the user list or in owner and assignment pickers;
- records it creates or changes show the integration as the creator.

To change what the integration can do, edit the client and choose a different role, or change the role itself in **Roles & Permissions**.

## Manage a client

Open the **⋮** menu on a client:

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Edit** | Change the name, scopes, role, token lifetime or rate limit |
| **Authorisations** | See who or what the client is acting as, when it was last used, and revoke an authorisation |
| **Rotate secret** | Creates a new secret and shows it once. The old secret keeps working for 24 hours so the other system can switch over without downtime |
| **Suspend** | The client cannot get new tokens, and tokens it already holds stop working within a minute. You can reactivate it |
| **Revoke** | Permanent. Every authorisation and token is cancelled, the integration user is deactivated, and the client can never be used again. Records it created keep their history |

If a secret may have leaked, rotate it at once. If you think the system itself is compromised, suspend or revoke the client.

## When a person connects an app

For a client that acts for a signed-in user, the person sees a PearlCRM screen naming the app and listing what it will be able to do, and chooses **Allow** or **Cancel**. The list only includes what their own role allows, and the app can never do more than their role allows, however its scopes are set. Support users cannot connect apps.

Each person can see and disconnect the apps they have connected under **Settings → Connected apps**. As an administrator you can see every authorisation for a client, and revoke any of them, from **Authorisations** in the client's menu.

## Activity

The **Activity** tab lists what happened to your clients: created, changed, secret rotated, suspended, revoked, people connecting and disconnecting apps, tokens issued (at most once an hour per authorisation), reused refresh tokens (which PearlCRM treats as theft and revokes), and failed sign-in attempts with the address they came from.

The clients table also shows each client's API calls over the last 30 days.

## For the developer

Send the developer building the integration the client ID, the secret (through a secure channel), and a link to the [API overview](/developers/overview).
