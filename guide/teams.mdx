A team is a named group of people. Teams exist so that a role can say "everyone in this person's team" without you listing names, and so access keeps up with the org chart as it changes.

## Nesting

A team can sit underneath another team. That nesting is what separates the two team options in the permission grid.

| Setting | Who it includes |
| --- | --- |
| **Team** | People in the same team as this person, and nobody below. |
| **+ Sub-teams** | The same, plus everyone in teams nested underneath. |

Access flows downward only. A manager sitting in a parent team reaches everyone beneath them. Someone in a sub-team never gains access to the parent team by being nested under it.

<Note>
A worked example. If Sales has two sub-teams, North and South, then a person in Sales with **+ Sub-teams** sees both. A person in North with **+ Sub-teams** sees only North, because nothing is nested below it.
</Note>

## Creating a team

<Steps>
  <Step title="Press New team">
    Give it a name people will recognise from the org chart.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a parent, or leave it at the top">
    A parent makes this team reachable by anyone above it using + Sub-teams.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add members">
    Type a name into the picker. People can belong to more than one team.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Changing the structure later

Moving a team under a different parent immediately changes who can see its records. Everyone above the new parent gains access, and everyone above the old one loses it.

Pearl refuses two changes outright. A team cannot become its own ancestor, and nesting cannot go more than ten levels deep.

<Warning>
A team must be empty before it can be deleted. This is deliberate: removing a team that still has members would silently change what those people can see, with no obvious cause to trace later.
</Warning>

## Teams and reporting lines

Teams drive access. The manager field on a person's profile does not.

If someone was moved to a different manager after their account was created, the team structure may still reflect the old arrangement. When a manager reports that they cannot see their team's records, this is the first thing to check.
